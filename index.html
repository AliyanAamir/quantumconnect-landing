Every network starts with a handshake.

Before two machines trade a single byte, they agree on who speaks, how fast, and what counts as heard. People and never got that step. Requests go in, answers come out, and nobody checks that both sides meant the same thing.

qconnect is the missing : a shared layer where intent, context and consent are confirmed in both directions before work begins, and every exchange leaves a you can read later.

Four things travel with every request.

Said twice, once by each side.

You say what you want in your own words. The SI says it back in its own, and you correct it before anything runs. Misreadings die here, where they cost nothing.

A scope you can read.

What the SI may see is a short list: these files, this thread, nothing from last Tuesday. Scope is visible, narrow by default, and widened only by you.

Stops before it spends.

Anything that costs money, sends a message or touches another person pauses and asks. Approval is a click on the exact action, never a blanket yes.

A record in plain words.

Each exchange writes down what was asked, what was understood and what was done. Replay it, hand it to a colleague, or delete it.

Three messages, then trust.
